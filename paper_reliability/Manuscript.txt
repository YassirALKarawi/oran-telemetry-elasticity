         ABSTRACT Resource elasticity under delayed telemetry can reduce provisioning cost while increasing
         deadline violations. This study examines that trade-off in an Open Radio Access Network (O-RAN)-
         motivated, three-stage service chain. A causal controller projects reported queues using applied actions
         and pending commands, then selects capacity and class reservations on a finite grid. A headroom parameter
         is calibrated separately under a common classwise mean-miss target of 0.1%. The experiment comprises
         336 calibration and 840 held-out executions across seven settings, with twenty independent test seeds
         per setting. Comparisons include fixed capacity, target-utilization sizing, a queue-weighted cost controller
         and component ablations. Three fixed-reference comparisons satisfy the complete descriptive reliability
         screen and reduce mean incremental cost by 35.61–37.73%. Markov-modulated traffic, compound arrivals
         and fifty-slot actuation expose failures of the selected policy. Derived sensitivity relations explain how
         headroom and demand uncertainty affect surrogate feasibility, while paired intervals quantify observed
         cost differences. The results establish conditional advantages within a synthetic packet-service model.
         They do not certify rare-event reliability or validate a physical O-RAN implementation.

         resource elasticity, reproducible simulation.

VIII. Discussion and Deployment Boundary
The supported advantage is conditional. P and R test repeata-
bility within the calibration traffic constructions, while J tests
one announced-delay law. Failures in M, B and L prevent
extrapolation to general workload or latency robustness.
Integer reservations, headroom and ledger replay explain
how decisions are formed. Their consistency with aggre-
gate results does not identify forecast accuracy or establish
packet-level guarantees.
